空间运镜类（空间穿梭）

指通过镜头的平移、推进、拉远、环绕、跟随等连续运镜方式，在同一空间内或不同空间之间实现自然衔接与无缝穿梭/切换，从而营造沉浸感、空间感和连续叙事效果

Case展示

空间运镜类提示词编写技巧：

编写结构

【镜头运动方式】+【下一张图片的空间元素】+【环境氛围】+（可选情绪/动作）

ps：一定要加镜头运动方式！但如果图片之间的信息足够，甚至可以无提示词；但是这对图片的要求极高，如果图片关联性比较少老老实实多写点过渡提示词吧～

基础运镜（适合任何场景）

镜头拉近 / 镜头拉远 / 固定镜头 / 镜头水平向右平移 / 镜头水平向左平移 / 镜头垂直向上移动 / 镜头垂直向下移动 / 镜头俯视拍摄（从高处向下看）/ 镜头仰视拍摄（从低处向上看）/ 镜头旋转进入下一场景 / 镜头高速穿越 / 镜头360度环绕 / 镜头俯冲 / 镜头拔升 / 镜头穿梭 / 镜头贴地飞行

如果有主体

镜头跟随主体向 + 方向运动 / 镜头360度环绕主体 / 镜头从主体背后穿越到正面 / 镜头缓慢推进并聚焦主体细节

注意事项

必须有镜头动作：如果要一镜到底，用“镜头+动作”开头，不要只描述场景，这样才能保持空间衔接

动作与秒数匹配：大幅运动 4-6s，小幅运动 1-3s，避免闪跳

镜头不要堆太多：一条提示词 1-2 种镜头动作即可

氛围词适量：简洁直观，不堆砌形容词

示例提示词

镜头缓慢推进，穿过雾气笼罩的走廊，环绕一圈后向上抬升至屋顶俯瞰城市

镜头高空俯视缓缓下降，顺着河道滑行，最终穿过一扇发光的拱门进入另一空间

镜头低角度仰视推进，掠过树梢与屋檐，镜头旋转后透过窗户进入昏暗室内

镜头匀速平移，跟随飘落的树叶，穿过长廊尽头的门缝，进入烛光摇曳的房间

镜头快速推进，越过桌面与书本，镜头环绕人物一周后缓缓拉远至夜色街道

角色动态类（复杂主体动态）

以角色的连续动作为核心，通过多帧展现人物在同一或不同场景中的动作变化。强调主体明确、动作连贯、与环境互动，常配合运镜实现自然衔接与沉浸感

Case展示

角色运动类提示词编写技巧：

编写结构

【角色身份/外观】 + 【关键动作】 + 【环境元素】 + 【镜头运动方式】 + （可选情绪 / 氛围）

ps：建议每一帧也加上运镜提示词过渡，运镜提示词撰写可看「运镜空间类」下方的内容

注意事项

动作连贯：角色在相邻帧中动作最好有延续性（例如：奔跑 - 跃起 - 落地，有逻辑的动作）

与环境互动：让角色动作与背景元素产生关联（跳在车顶上、推开门等）

动作和镜头匹配：快节奏动作配快速运镜，慢节奏动作配缓慢推进或环绕

主体清晰：确保角色是画面视觉中心，避免与背景杂乱元素抢夺注意力

秒数合理：大动作 3-6s，小动作 1-3s

常见 Badcase

动作无连接：第一帧奔跑，第二帧突然坐下无过渡

缺少镜头运动：只写“男子挥剑”而无“镜头拉近”或“镜头环绕”

角色不突出：背景细节过多导致主体被淹没

身披黑袍的剑客猛然拔剑，镜头低角度跟随疾冲穿过昏暗长廊

叙事类

以长镜头贯穿故事，将空间运镜、角色动态、节奏变化等多种镜头语言融合成一体，让画面在时间与空间上连续推进，完整呈现情节发展

Case展示

首先！再重点强调一下，什么是叙事类的长视频：

叙事类是一种以长镜头推动情节发展的多帧视频创作方式，在一镜到底的连贯运动中，将角色动态、空间运镜、节奏变化等多种元素融合，呈现完整的故事走向。它常用于电影、广告、短片等需要沉浸感与情绪递进的场景，通过镜头的连续性与画面细节的变化，引导观众自然地进入故事。所以它其实是以上场景的结合版本

叙事类提示词编写技巧：

编写结构：

【镜头运动方式】 + 【当前画面内容】 + 【过渡方式/动作】 + 【下一画面内容】 + 【氛围/情绪】

编写要点：

故事主线先定好：先明确“这个长镜头的故事逻辑”，按节奏顺序去排帧，顺序很重要

多类别融合：在不同帧用不同的镜头语言（运镜 / 动态 / 节奏 / 产品特写等）

过渡自然：帧与帧的连接要有空间或动作的合理性，避免“突变”

情绪递进：前后帧的情绪最好逐步加强或缓和，制造观感节奏

注意事项

不要没有故事逻辑：只是随机拼接几个画面，用户会觉得割裂

过渡无空间 / 动作衔接：比如室内画面突然硬切到室外，缺少镜头运动承接

情绪跳跃太猛：平静场景瞬间切到极端激烈的动作，没有铺垫会显得突兀

循环动画：

首帧和尾帧保证图片一样就可以了，可以成为循环壁纸

写提示词总结（非常重要）

编写提示词的关键要素：

明确镜头运动方式：
在写提示词时，始终要明确镜头的运动方式（如：拉近、拉远、平移、旋转等）。镜头运动方式是智能多帧生成的基础，能够影响的流畅性以及视觉效果

简洁且有针对性的描述：
重点描述场景中的关键元素（如：人物、物品、背景等），避免过度细化无关细节，尤其是对于背景和环境的描述要简洁、清晰。确保每个镜头的提示词都有明确的指引，而不是冗余的描述。但是不能啥也不写，目前模型还是需要清晰提示词的引导

注重情感和动作的衔接：
在动态场景（如角色动态、节奏驱动）中，要精确地描写人物的动作与情感，避免模糊的描述。运镜的提示词应当配合情节进展，确保视觉语言的连贯性

根据场景类型调整提示词：
每种场景类型（如空间运镜、产品展示、叙事等）都有不同的提示词要求。对于空间运镜类，着重描述镜头的空间穿梭和过渡；对于产品展示类，则更注重产品细节的呈现和光影的变化；对于叙事类，需注重人物的情感、动作及场景变化的流畅度

不建议不写提示词：

此项能力对关键帧要求比较高，关键帧越好视频效果就越好，建议用户一定要打磨好图片，不写提示词前提在于图片信息连续足够；如果没有足够的信息，一定要写精准的提示词！！！

小提示

虽然我们提供了几种场景和方法，但实际上，独特的创造力才是最重要的部分。不要局限于以上提到的这些，尽情发挥，去探索更多可能性。长镜头的魅力就在于它能够带来无尽的创意空间，给你带来更丰富的表现形式。无论是叙事、动效，还是虚拟与现实的结合，只要你想，任何创意都可以通过智能多帧来实现。现在，尽情去挑战更多场景吧～

Spatial Camera Movement (Spatial Shuttling)

refers to the continuous camera movements such as panning, zooming in, zooming out, orbiting, and following, which achieve natural connection and seamless shuttling/switching within the same space or between different spaces, thereby creating a sense of immersion, spatiality, and continuous narrative effect

Case Display

Writing Techniques for Spatial Camera Movement Prompts:

Writing Structure

[Camera movement mode] + [Spatial elements of the next image] + [Environmental atmosphere] + (Optional emotion/action)

PS: Be sure to add the camera movement method! However, if the information between images is sufficient, it may even be possible to have no cue words; but this places extremely high demands on the images. If the images have relatively little relevance, it's better to write more transition cue words!

Basic camera movement (suitable for any scenario)

Zoom in / Zoom out / Fixed shot / Pan horizontally to the right / Pan horizontally to the left / Move vertically upward / Move vertically downward / Shoot from a bird's-eye view (looking down from a high place) / Shoot from a worm's-eye view (looking up from a low place) / Rotate the camera to transition to the next scene / Zoom through at high speed / 360-degree camera rotation / Dive / Ascend / Shuttle / Fly close to the ground

If there is a subject

The camera follows the subject moving in the + direction / The camera orbits the subject 360 degrees / The camera passes from behind the subject to the front / The camera slowly advances and focuses on the subject's details

Precautions

There must be camera movements: If you want to have a single continuous shot, start with "camera + movement" instead of just describing the scene, so as to maintain spatial continuity

Action matches seconds: large movements 4-6s, small movements 1-3s, avoid flashing and jumping

Don't stack too many camera shots: one prompt only requires 1-2 camera movements

Appropriate use of atmosphere words: concise and intuitive, without piling up adjectives

Example prompt

The camera slowly advances, passing through the fog-shrouded corridor, making a full circle, then rising up to the roof to overlook the city

The camera slowly descends from a high altitude, glides along the river channel, and finally passes through a glowing archway into another space

The camera moves in with a low-angle upward shot, skimming over treetops and eaves, then rotates and enters the dim interior through a window

The camera pans smoothly, following the falling leaves, passing through the crack in the door at the end of the corridor, and entering the room with flickering candles

The camera quickly advances, passing over the desktop and books, circles around the character once, and then slowly pulls back to the night street

Role Dynamics Class (Complex Subject Dynamics)

Centering around the continuous actions of the character, it showcases the character's action changes in the same or different scenes through multiple frames. It emphasizes a clear subject, coherent actions, and interaction with the environment, often coordinating with camera movements to achieve natural transitions and immersion.

Case Display

Writing Techniques for Role Movement Class Prompts:

Writing Structure

[Character Identity / Appearance] + [Key Action] + [Environmental Element] + [Camera Movement Style] + (Optional Mood / Atmosphere)

PS: It is recommended to add camera movement cue transitions to each frame as well. For writing camera movement cues, please refer to the content under "Camera Movement Space Category".

Precautions

Action Continuity: The actions of the character in adjacent frames should preferably have continuity (e.g., running - leaping - landing, logical actions)

Interaction with the environment: Make the character's actions correlate with background elements (such as jumping on the car roof, pushing open the door, etc.)

Action and shot matching: fast-paced actions are paired with rapid camera movements, while slow-paced actions are paired with slow panning or circling

Clear Subject: Ensure that the character is the visual center of the frame, avoiding competition for attention with cluttered elements in the background

Reasonable duration: 3-6s for large movements, 1-3s for small movements

Common Badcase

Actions are not connected: running in the first frame, suddenly sitting down without transition in the second frame

Lack of camera movement: Only writing "a man swings his sword" without "camera zoom in" or "camera pan around"

The role is not prominent: excessive background details cause the subject to be submerged

Example prompt:

The black-robed swordsman suddenly drew his sword, and the camera followed his rapid dash through the dim corridor from a low angle

The red-dressed woman slowly raises her hand to push open the wooden door. The camera moves closer to capture the details of her hand and then smoothly pulls back to a full view.

A young man leaps onto the rooftop, with the camera circling his figure 360 degrees as fireworks burst in the night sky in the background

The masked dancer spins and waves the ribbon, and the camera pans through the air entangled with the ribbon into the next scene

Product Display (Commercial Product Advertisement)

Focusing on highlighting the product, it presents the product's appearance details, functional features, and usage scenarios through multiple frames

Case Display

Writing Techniques for Product Display Prompts:

Writing Structure

[Shot Movement Method] + [Product Part/Feature] + [Display Detail/Function] + [Environmental Atmosphere/Texture]

PS: It is recommended to add camera movement cue transitions to each frame as well. For writing camera movement cues, please refer to the content under "Camera Movement Space Category".

Common camera movement methods (supplementary for product display, details still refer to the content of spatial movement)

The camera rotates 360 degrees around the product / The camera slides along the edge of the product / The camera zooms in to focus on the logo or key parts / The camera passes through the surface elements of the product and switches to another part

Precautions

Details and proportions of the product: Note that when creating the image, try to keep the details and proportions of the product the same as much as possible; otherwise, issues such as jumps, deformations, and logo disappearance may occur.

Physical changes occur gradually: In product displays, phenomena such as liquid flow and texture changes often occur, which can be described with terms like "slowly" and "gradually" to avoid sudden deformation

Create variation with camera movements: It is recommended to use basic camera movement prompts to transition the shot to a new perspective rather than directly changing the scene

Example prompt

The camera slowly zooms in, sliding along the arc of the metal casing, and cuts to a close-up of the logo, with the metallic texture shining under the soft light

Fixed shot, side shot of the flower, the flower slowly blooms, the flower slowly turns to the front, and the product is displayed

The camera slowly zooms in on the watch face, and the digital scale glows and gradually transitions to the next scene

The camera rotates around the coffee cup, and the surface of the coffee in the cup transforms into golden waves that extend to the next frame

The camera zooms in on the mouth of the red wine bottle, and the red wine liquid transforms into a flowing silk image in the air

Narrative

Using long takes to run through the story, it integrates various cinematic languages such as spatial camera movements, character dynamics, and rhythm changes into one, enabling the visuals to progress continuously in time and space, and fully presenting the development of the plot

Case Display

First! Let me emphasize again what narrative long-form videos are:

Narrative is a multi-frame video creation method that advances the plot through long takes, integrating various elements such as character dynamics, spatial camera movements, and rhythm changes in a continuous one-shot movement to present a complete story arc. It is commonly used in scenarios such as movies, advertisements, and short films that require immersion and emotional progression, guiding the audience to naturally enter the story through the continuity of the shots and the changes in visual details. Writing Structure:

[Shot Movement Method] + [Current Frame Content] + [Transition Method/Action] + [Next Frame Content] + [Atmosphere/Emotion]

Writing Key Points:

First, define the main storyline: clearly identify "the story logic of this long take" first, and then arrange the frames in the order of rhythm, as the order is crucial

Multi-category Fusion: Use different shot languages (camera movement / dynamics / rhythm / product close-up, etc.) in different frames

Natural Transition: The connection between frames should have spatial or action rationality to avoid "abrupt changes".

Emotional Progression: The emotions in consecutive frames should preferably gradually intensify or ease, creating a rhythm for visual perception

Precautions

Don't lack story logic: simply randomly splicing a few scenes will make users feel disjointed

Transition without space / Action connection: For example, when an indoor scene suddenly cuts abruptly to an outdoor scene, lacking the connection of camera movement

Emotional leaps are too abrupt: a sudden transition from a calm scene to extremely intense action without any build-up will appear jarring

Loop Animation:

Key elements of writing prompts:

Specify the camera movement:
When writing prompts, always specify the camera movement (e.g., zoom in, zoom out, pan, rotate, etc.). Camera movement is the foundation of intelligent multi-frame generation and can affect the smoothness and visual effects.

Concise and targeted description:
Focus on describing the key elements in the scene (e.g., characters, objects, background, etc.), avoiding over-refinement of irrelevant details. In particular, descriptions of the background and environment should be concise and clear. Ensure that the prompt for each shot provides clear guidance rather than redundant descriptions. However, do not leave it blank, as the current model still requires clear prompts for guidance.

Focus on the connection between emotion and action: 
In dynamic scenes (such as character dynamics, rhythm-driven), it is necessary to accurately describe the actions and emotions of characters, avoiding vague descriptions. The prompting words for camera movements should be coordinated with the plot progression to ensure the coherence of visual language

Adjust prompts according to scene type:
Each scene type (such as spatial camera movement, product display, narrative, etc.) has different prompt requirements. For spatial camera movement, focus on describing the spatial traversal and transition of the camera; for product display, pay more attention to the presentation of product details and changes in light and shadow; for narrative, focus on the smoothness of characters' emotions, actions, and scene changes
